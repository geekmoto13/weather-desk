A PATH FROM YOUR STATION

Radio prediction setup

Your transmitting location comes from the dashboard’s saved coordinates, rounded to approximately 1 km. The selected provider (KC2G or the HamClock.com VOACAP service) receives approximate path coordinates, mode and power. PSK Reporter receives only the selected callsign. KC2G uses your gain/noise settings. VOACAP uses fixed 0 dBi model antennas, a 3° minimum takeoff angle and its own noise assumptions. Both are models of propagation, not guarantees of a contact.

No grid needed for listed locations. DXCC entries use representative points; choose a regional city for a more specific path.

SNR is predicted in the selected mode’s bandwidth. It is not a measured on-air report. The model’s reference frequencies represent bands; they are not operating-frequency or licence advice. The lowest usable band means the lowest tested frequency at 50% or greater circuit reliability. A true continuous LUF needs a fuller frequency analysis.

A note about the readings

Automatic mode uses your connected Ambient station first. Otherwise it chooses the nearest usable CWOP report within 75 km through NOAA MADIS, with an official NOAA station as a fallback. Every selected public station must report measured precipitation as well as a recent usable temperature. A valid zero qualifies; missing or rejected rain values do not. If none qualifies, no temperature-only station is selected. CWOP requests are cached for ten minutes; official NOAA observations for five minutes. Actual observation times, station identifiers and distances are shown. Public reports older than 90 minutes are marked stale. Personal station readings retain the 15-minute stale threshold. MADIS quality checks apply separately to each sensor. Altimeter pressure is labelled QNH and is not the same as sea-level pressure. Rolling 24-hour rainfall is labelled with that period and is never added into daily or annual counters.

Temperature calendar test: a completed day’s mean is (high + low) ÷ 2. Red is above the historical upper-third cutoff; blue below the lower-third cutoff; green is the middle third, including both endpoints. Cutoffs are the empirical 33⅓ and 66⅔ percentiles of paired daily means for the same date in 1991–2020, using linear interpolation. At least 24 of 30 years are required (7 of 8 leap days). This coverage rule is our test choice. Click a day for cutoffs, reference coverage, average and exact departure. This is an experimental daily adaptation of CPC outlook categories, not an official NOAA daily tolerance. Today, incomplete days and days without sufficient history stay uncoloured. Reference thresholds and personal station observations are saved locally. The existing daily normal is a separate comparison and may differ from the midpoint of the green range.

Feels like & dew point: Ambient’s values are preferred. If missing, dew point uses the Magnus approximation, while feels like uses the NWS wind-chill or heat-index calculation where applicable, otherwise air temperature.

Beaufort: the number and description beside the wind are the traditional 0–12 scale equivalent of your current wind speed. The formal scale normally uses a 10-minute average measured 10 metres above ground, so this is a station-speed equivalent, not a separate official Beaufort observation. Gusts are shown separately.

Daily max gust: the highest gust reported by your station since its daily reset, shown in mph and km/h. It uses Ambient’s daily counter; if the field is missing, the value stays unavailable.

Relative pressure: your station’s calibrated relative pressure, converted from inHg to hPa. The arrow compares it with an observation about three hours earlier. A change larger than 0.5 hPa is rising or falling; a smaller change is shown as steady. Without an older reading, it says “building history.” The three-hour window follows the usual meteorological pressure-tendency convention. The ±0.5 hPa steady range is this dashboard’s display threshold; this simple net change is not the full official tendency code, which also describes the shape of the pressure change.

Rain: daily, event, weekly and monthly totals are Ambient’s station counters. If Ambient supplies its yearly counter, it is shown directly. Otherwise YTD adds one completed monthly counter for each previous month, then the live current month to date. Today’s rain is already included. Month-end windows are fetched once and saved; the daily/lifetime consistency check from older versions is no longer used. Click Year to date for a monthly breakdown and observation times. A missing monthly counter is identified and retried; it is never treated as zero. The first import needs one query per completed month, with normal API pacing. The rain rate uses Ambient’s hourlyrainin field.

Calendar: month-to-date covers the first of this month through today; year-to-date covers January 1 through today. Station totals, normals and historical extremes use the same cutoff date. A reported annual counter needs the January reset; the calculated total always starts January 1. NOAA precipitation is liquid water, including melted snow; an unheated home rain gauge may not capture snowfall equivalently. Snowfall depth itself is a different measurement. No June-to-June season is mixed into these comparisons.

Lightning: nearby flash positions and occurrence times come from NOAA GOES-18 or GOES-19 GLM. Recent observations are cached to speed subsequent loads. Missing values and quality flags are checked before displaying a flash. Distances and compass directions use your saved coordinates, not a remote public weather station. The closest accepted flash is selected within 75 km during the 45-minute window ending at the displayed satellite data time. A partial load says nearest loaded flash; complete, fresh, gap-free coverage is required before showing No detections. Quality warnings, failed downloads and stale data are not all-clear messages. Satellite flashes include cloud lightning and do not identify an exact ground-strike location. The Blitzortung live map is a separate source and can show different activity. It uses the provider’s documented iframe, not its restricted raw feed. NOAA data need no key. GOES data are checked every minute; observations beyond five minutes old are marked delayed. The supported area excludes locations above 52° latitude and outside the Americas/nearby Pacific longitude range. The earlier Weatherbit connection is no longer used, and no paid Weatherbit requests are made. Map and decoder libraries are bundled or linked with their attribution. NOAA GLM · EUMETSAT LI.

Sky: sunrise, sunset, moonrise, moonset and moon illumination are approximate astronomical calculations for your selected location and local calendar day. Buildings, terrain and atmospheric conditions can shift the visible rise and set. Solar radiation does not determine cloud cover.

Tides: NOAA predictions for the named coastal station, in feet above Mean Lower Low Water (MLLW). A nearby harbor’s timing and heights can differ from your beach. Predictions do not include the actual weather-driven water-level changes.

Forecast beacon: a Berkeley Building-inspired summary of the prevailing weather over the next 12 hours. Each forecast condition counts for the time it covers; the category with the most hours sets the light. Steady blue means clear/mostly clear or sunny/mostly sunny; blinking blue means clouds; steady red means rain; blinking red means snow/ice. Partly cloudy and partly sunny count toward clouds. For example, 9 clear hours and 3 cloudy hours give steady blue. The small hour breakdown includes brief rain or snow chances even when they do not win. Exact ties favor snow/ice, then rain, then clouds, then clear. It uses the separate NOAA/NWS hourly point forecast for your saved coordinates, not the station supplying climate records. The source line names the forecast office; the NWS hourly forecast link opens the official forecast for that point. If the summary only mentions frost, heat, cold or wind, the accompanying NWS sky icon supplies clear/cloudy conditions when available. Frost is noted separately and does not mean falling snow or freezing rain. Rain/snow hours require at least a 30% precipitation chance when NWS supplies one. Gray means the forecast is old, unavailable, or does not cover the full 12 hours. Sample forecasts are labeled. This is your own forecast indicator; the actual building’s beacon and Red Sox game-status light are separate. Motion follows your device’s reduced-motion preference.

Sky colors: automatic colors use solar altitude for dawn, day, sunset and night. A recent low solar-radiation reading softens the daytime palette; it does not measure cloud cover. The Sky colors menu also lets you preview each palette.

History: the latest 288 historical records are requested from Ambient. Only readings within the last 24 hours appear on the chart, which leaves gaps over 45 minutes. Chart observations are kept locally for up to 40 days while the dashboard runs. A separate monthly cache preserves completed monthly rain counters for the calendar-year sum.

Local climate references: location search chooses a nearby reporting climate station with long daily temperature and precipitation records and verified 1991–2020 normals. Its name appears beside the records and in the calendar. It may be a different site from your current-weather station. Existing Lynn installations retain the Boston Area ThreadEx reference. Daily record ties are included. Precipitation records and normals use matching month-to-date and January-1-to-date windows. Incomplete historical precipitation windows are excluded; trace amounts remain labelled. Current station totals are compared with full-day normals through today, so the arrows can change during the day.

Saved station: Remember saves the API key, application key and selected MAC in this app’s private folder. Windows encrypts the saved connection for your Windows account. Mac/Linux use a local file with owner-only permissions. Uncheck Remember for a session-only connection; Forget station removes the saved connection.

Keys are never included in exported images, weather text, or saved observations. Demo readings stay separate from real observations.
